Privacy Policy

Personal Health MCP
Last updated: September 7, 2026

Personal Health MCP is a private, owner-operated service with no public user registration. It provides the owner with read-only, on-demand access to the owner's Google Health data through explicitly approved MCP-compatible clients.

1. Data accessed

The service may access the following Google Health data only when the owner grants the corresponding permission:

  • sleep timing, duration, stages, and related processing information;
  • health metrics and measurements, such as heart rate, heart-rate variability, respiratory information, oxygen saturation, and supported temperature measurements;
  • activity and fitness information, such as steps, active time, exercise, energy expenditure, and related activity summaries; and
  • settings and paired-device metadata, including available synchronization timestamps used to explain data freshness.

Availability varies by device, source, Google Health support, and the permissions granted. The service does not request or perform Google Health write access.

2. How data is used

Google Health data is used only to provide owner-requested health and fitness features visible in the authorized client's interface. These features include retrieving measurements, preparing summaries, comparing bounded periods, identifying missing or stale observations, and supplying evidence for the owner's own health and fitness review.

The service distinguishes measurements from interpretation. It does not use Google Health data for advertising, profiling, credit or lending decisions, medical-device functions, or human-subject research, and it does not present an automated medical diagnosis.

3. Processing, storage, and security

Health information is retrieved on demand from Google Health, processed transiently by a service running on Cloudflare Workers, and returned to the owner's authorized MCP client. The service does not maintain a persistent archive or warehouse of raw health measurements.

Google OAuth tokens, MCP authorization records, and minimal non-health security state are retained server-side only as needed to operate and protect approved connections. Access is restricted by the configured Google account owner, approved MCP grants, read-only scope mapping, and audited data-type and operation allowlists. Requests, time ranges, pagination, and response sizes are bounded. Application logging is designed to exclude health payloads and credentials.

4. Data sharing and service providers

Data flows from Google Health through the Cloudflare-hosted service to the MCP client that the owner explicitly chooses and authorizes. Authorized clients may include OpenAI services such as ChatGPT or Codex. Google, Cloudflare, and the selected client process information under their own applicable terms, privacy policies, account settings, and retention controls.

Once a result reaches an authorized client, that client's storage or reuse is outside this service's direct control. The owner should manage conversations, saved content, memory, and retention using the receiving client's controls.

The service does not sell Google Health data or share it with advertising platforms, data brokers, information resellers, or unauthorized clients. No additional third-party health-data forwarding is performed.

5. Retention and deletion

Raw health responses are not retained as a persistent service-side history after the request is processed. Authentication credentials remain only while needed for an active connection or until they expire, are replaced, or are disconnected.

Disconnecting an MCP client removes that client's active local grant and tokens. A minimal non-health revocation marker may be retained for up to 31 days to prevent reuse of revoked credentials. Reconnecting replaces the previous grant for the same owner and client.

A client disconnect does not delete data already delivered to that client and does not revoke unrelated client grants. The owner may separately revoke the project's Google Account access to end Google authorization across the project's connections.

6. Owner choices and controls

  • The owner chooses which supported Google Health permissions to grant.
  • Missing or declined optional permissions are reported rather than silently replaced with normal or zero values.
  • The owner can disconnect an MCP client to revoke that client's service grant.
  • The owner can revoke the application's Google access from Google Account settings.
  • Data retained by a receiving client must be managed through that client's account and privacy controls.

7. Google Health API Limited Use

The use of information received from the Google Health API and/or Developer Tools will adhere to the Google Health API Developer and User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

8. Changes and contact

This policy will be updated before the service materially changes how it accesses, uses, stores, or shares Google Health data. Privacy or support questions may be submitted through the public resources repository issue tracker. Do not include health data, credentials, or other sensitive information in a public issue.
